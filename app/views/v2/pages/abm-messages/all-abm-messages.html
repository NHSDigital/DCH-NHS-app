Health Visitor

Your health visitor appointment is soon.

A health visitor is a qualified nurse or midwife who has had extra training. They're there to help you and your new baby.

Every parent is seen by a health visitor. They will normally visit you at your home around 10 days after your baby is born.

You can talk to your health visitor about how things are going and ask any questions you have.

They can help you with things like: * bonding with your baby
* your mental health
* vaccinations and screening tests
* feeding your baby

To get the most from your appointment, it's helpful to prepare a list of questions or concerns about your health or your baby's development.

You can also arrange for a family member or friend to be with you for the visit.

If you have not been contacted about an appointment contact your health visiting team or your midwife.

You do not need to wait for this appointment if you are worried about something.

Contact your GP practice straight away if you have any concerns.

Vitamins

Vitamins help your baby's healthy development. The NHS recommends breastfed babies have vitamin D supplements from birth.

All children aged 6 months to 5 years are recommended to take daily vitamins of A, C and D.

Babies who have 500ml or more of formula a day do not need extra vitamins until they are having less formula.

Be careful not to double dose. Having too many of some vitamins can be harmful.

If your child has already been prescribed vitamins, consult your health visitor, doctor or pharmacist.
