Why vitamins matter

Babies and young children may not get enough vitamins A, C and D, even if they feed well. Vitamins help babies grow and stay healthy. Taking vitamins is sometimes called 'vitamin supplementation'.

Advice on vitamins for babies and young children

All children aged 6 months to 5 years are recommended to take daily vitamins of A, C and D.

Babies who have 500ml or more of formula a day do not need extra vitamins until they are having less formula.

You can buy vitamins at your local pharmacy. If you are receiving NHS Healthy Start payments, you can get these vitamins for free.

Questions about giving vitamins

Before giving your baby vitamin supplements, speak to your pharmacist or health visitor for advice on which vitamin you should give your child.

Remember, supplements are not a replacement for healthy eating habits.

Be careful not to double dose

Having too much of some vitamins can be harmful.

Keep to the dose recommended on the label, and be careful not to give your child 2 supplements at the same time.
