How to start introducing solid foods

When should my baby start solid foods?

The NHS recommends introducing solid foods when babies are around 6 months old.

How much food does my baby need

In the beginning, your baby will only need a small amount of food each day. Don't worry about how much they eat.

The important thing is trying new foods and flavours and learning to swallow solid food.

Does my baby still need milk?

Yes, breastmilk or infant formula should be your baby's main drink until they are 12 months old.

As time goes on and your baby eats more solids, they may naturally want less breast milk or infant formula.

Remember your baby's tummy is tiny and fills up quickly. Offer milk feeds after solids.

What if my baby doesn't want to eat?

It may take 10 tries or even more for your baby to get used to new foods, flavours and textures.

There will be days when they eat more, some when they eat less. Some days they may reject everything. Don't worry – it's normal.

Just keep offering a variety of foods – even the ones they don't seem to like – and let them get used to it in their own time.

How much sugar should my baby eat?

Babies don't need sweet or salty snacks or treats.

Sweet drinks like squash, fizzy drinks, milkshakes and fruit juice can have lots of sugar too. Avoid these to help prevent tooth decay.

Introducing foods that could trigger allergies

Foods most likely to cause allergies (such as peanuts, eggs, or fish) can be introduced one at a time from around six months.

Remember, peanuts should be crushed or ground to avoid choking.
