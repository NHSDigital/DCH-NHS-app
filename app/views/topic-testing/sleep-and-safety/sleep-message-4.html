Keeping your baby safe from falls

Babies learn to wriggle and kick very quickly.

It's not long before they can roll off beds and changing tables.

Any child can have an accident, but children are more at risk if they:

  • are boys (boys are more likely to have accidents than girls).
  • are left unattended on raised surfaces like beds, sofas or changing tables.
  • are placed in bouncing cradles or car seats on raised surfaces like kitchen worktops or tables.

How to prevent falls

There are simple things you can do to lower the risk of falls.

Use the floor

  • Change your baby's nappy on a changing mat on the floor.
  • Always keep bouncing cradles or baby car seats on the floor, as your baby's wriggling could tip it over the edge.

If you have stairs

  • Keep steps clear of clutter, toys or any other objects.
  • Hold onto the handrail when carrying your baby up and down, and watch where you put your feet.

When your baby starts crawling

Once babies learn to crawl, they may try to climb onto things. This increases the risk of falling.

Make sure you:

  • fit safety gates to stop your baby getting onto stairs.
  • check that gaps between your banisters or railings are no wider than 6.5cm (2.5 inches), so a small baby cannot squeeze their body through.
  • remove cot toys and cot bumpers, as a baby can climb on them and may fall out of the cot.

Read NHS advice on baby and toddler safety.
