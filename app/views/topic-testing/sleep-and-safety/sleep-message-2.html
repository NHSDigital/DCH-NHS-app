How to co-sleep more safely

The safest place for a baby to sleep is in their own cot or Moses basket, free from anything that could cover their face and head.

However, you may find yourself sharing a bed with your baby, whether you planned to or fell asleep unintentionally.

If this happens, it is important to do it safely.

When it is not safe to co-sleep

You should never share a bed with your baby if:

  • you are extremely tired.
  • you or your partner smoke.
  • you have drunk alcohol or taken drugs, including medications that makes you drowsy.
  • your baby has a fever or any signs of illness.
  • your baby was born prematurely (before 37 weeks).
  • your baby has a low birth weight (under 2.5kg or 5.5lb).

Never fall asleep on a sofa or armchair with your baby.

This significantly increases the risk of sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS).

How to make bed-sharing safer

Adult beds have not been designed or safety tested for infant sleep in the same way as a cot or Moses basket.

If you do share your bed with your baby, make sure you:

  • always put your baby to sleep on their back, not their front or side.
  • don't bring other children or pets into bed with you.
  • check that your baby cannot fall out of bed, or become trapped between the mattress and the wall.
  • keep the bed clear of pillows, sheets, blankets or anything that could cover their face and head.
