Understanding your baby's sleep patterns

Your baby will have their own pattern of waking and sleeping, and it's unlikely to be the same as other babies you know.

Every baby is different. For example, some babies:

  • sleep much more than others.
  • sleep for long periods.
  • sleep in short bursts.
  • sleep through the night early on.
  • do not sleep through the night for a long time.

Allow yourself time to adjust to life with your newborn in the first few weeks.

Helping your baby to sleep

There are a few things you can do to help support your baby.

Establish a bedtime routine

A simple, soothing routine can help your baby wind down and signal that it's time to settle.

You could try:

  • giving your baby a warm bath.
  • changing into night clothes and a fresh nappy.
  • dimming the lights in the room.
  • putting your baby to bed.
  • reading a bedtime story.

As your child gets older, it can be helpful to keep to a similar bedtime routine.

Too much excitement and stimulation just before bedtime can wake your child up again.

Sleep patterns will keep changing

Your baby's sleep patterns will not stay the same. For example, growth spurts, teething and illnesses can all affect how your baby sleeps.

Be prepared to change routines as your baby grows and enters different stages.

Look after yourself

Lack of sleep can make you feel physically and emotionally drained. Try to remember that this stage will not last forever.

During this period, try to:

  • sleep or rest whenever your baby sleeps.
  • accept or ask for help from friends and family.
  • ignore any non-essential jobs around the house.
  • go outside for a walk and some fresh air.

Get additional help and support

If your baby is having problems sleeping or you need more advice about getting into a routine, speak to your health visitor.
