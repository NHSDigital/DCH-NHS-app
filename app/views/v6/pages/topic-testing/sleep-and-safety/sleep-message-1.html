Sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS) is the sudden and unexplained death of an apparently healthy baby under 1 year old. It used to be called cot death.

While it is rare, it does still happen.

SIDS is more likely to happen in the first 6 months of a baby's life. It can happen anytime a baby is asleep, day or night.

How to keep your baby safe

There are simple things you can do to lower the risk of SIDS.

Put your baby on their back to sleep

  • Always place your baby on their back to sleep, not their front or side
  • Use the "feet-to-foot" position, placing their feet at the bottom of the cot
  • Gently roll them onto their back if they roll over

Share a room with your baby

  • Put your baby in a separate cot or Moses basket
  • Keep your baby in your room for every sleep, day and night, for at least the first 6 months

Keep your baby's cot clear

  • Use a firm, flat mattress with a fitted sheet
  • Keep the cot clear of pillows, duvets, cot bumpers, loose bedding or anything that could cover their face and head
  • Avoid soft toys, sleep positioners, pods or nests

Keep a good room temperature

  • Aim to keep the room temperature between 16C and 20C
  • Check your baby's chest or the back of their neck to make sure they are not sweating or hot to touch
  • Take off hats and outdoor clothing as soon as you come indoors

Get additional help and support

The Lullaby Trust has additional information and advice on safer sleep for parents. They also provide support for families affected by sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS).

Visit The Lullaby Trust website for more information

Exposure to cigarette smoke or unsafe sleep environments (like sleeping on a sofa with your baby) also increases the risk.
