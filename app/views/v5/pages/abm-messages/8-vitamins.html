Advice on vitamins for babies and young children

Why vitamins matter

Babies and young children may not get enough vitamins A, C and D, even if they feed well.

Vitamins help babies grow and stay healthy. Taking vitamins is sometimes called 'vitamin supplementation'.

What vitamins to take

All children aged 6 months to 5 years are recommended to take daily vitamins of A, C and D.

Babies who have 500ml or more of formula milk a day do not need extra vitamins until they are having less formula milk.

You can buy vitamins at your local pharmacy.

If you are receiving NHS Healthy Start payments, you can get these vitamins for free.

Remember, supplements are not a replacement for healthy eating habits.

Be careful not to double dose

Having too much of some vitamins can be harmful.

Keep to the dose recommended on the label, and be careful not to give your child 2 supplements at the same time.

If your child has already been prescribed vitamins or has allergies or you want to check which supplements to give, consult your health visitor, doctor or pharmacist.
