GP Registration

If you or your child get sick, a GP (General Practitioner) or doctors' surgery is usually your first point of contact.

They can help you if your baby gets ill or if you are worried about their health. They can also refer you to other NHS services.

Health Visitor

A health visitor is a qualified nurse or midwife who has had extra training. It's their job to help you and your baby with things like:

  • your baby's development
  • feeding
  • bonding

Every parent is seen by a health visitor. They will normally visit you at home, around 10 days after your baby is born.

You will be contacted to confirm the appointment.

To get the most from your appointment, it's helpful to prepare a list of questions or concerns about your health or your baby's development.

Find more information about health visitors here: https://www.nhs.uk/services-for-parents

Weaning 1

Thinking about weaning? Don't begin too early.

The NHS recommends that you should introduce solid food from around 6 months old.

If your baby was premature, you may wish to discuss timing with a health visitor. If you are concerned about your baby's development or readiness to start solid foods, it is best to speak to your GP or health visitor.

Vitamins

Vitamins help your baby's healthy development.

All children aged 6 months to 5 years are recommended to take daily vitamins of A, C and D.

Babies who have 500ml or more of formula milk a day do not need extra vitamins until they are having less formula.

Be careful not to double dose. Too much of some vitamins can be harmful.

If your child has already been prescribed vitamins or has allergies or you want to check which supplements to give, consult your health visitor, doctor or pharmacist. You can find more NHS advice here: https://www.nhs.uk/baby/vitamins
